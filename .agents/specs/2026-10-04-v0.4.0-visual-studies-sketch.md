Status: sketch of the product and architectural discussion on 2026-10-04. Visual studies are the intended foundation of v0.4.0. The shared-state placement approach is a promising candidate pending a spike, not an approved implementation design. This document does not authorize implementation, paid inference, publication, or release.

## User problem and intended value

An author has an article with images and wants to understand whether those images do their job for the respondents the author cares about. The initial questions are whether an image earns interest in a deeper article read, distracts from the article, or complements its meaning. Attraction, understanding, and continuity are distinct outcomes: an image can succeed at one and fail at another.

The concrete example is an article with five sections and an image between sections 3 and 4. The author wants to know whether that image at that point adds to or takes away from the flow. The useful outcome is an editorial decision about keeping, removing, moving, replacing, captioning, or presenting the image, with respondent differences and trade-offs visible.

Candidate product framing: before publishing, explore whether the images help the readers you care about enter, understand, and continue through an article. These are modeled judgments that help an author investigate choices, not measurements of human gaze, actual reading completion, population response, or causal uplift.

## Related slices of value

### Assess an image in its authored position

Ask whether the existing image supports the surrounding argument and transition, reinforces the preceding idea, prepares the respondent for the next section, introduces misleading expectations, offers a useful pause, or interrupts continuity. After-reading judgments about complementarity and coherence differ from first-encounter judgments about expressed interest in continuing.

A staged journey could expose sections 1 through 3, the image, and then sections 4 and 5. Question placement requires care: asking an explicit question about the image can itself direct attention toward it and change subsequent modeled responses. This remains a possible study shape, not the required implementation approach.

### Compare hypothetical insertion points over shared state

The later discussion supplied an alternative that fits Sheg's existing independent-question groups. Supply the whole article, actual image, caption when relevant, respondent profile, and five explicitly defined insertion points as one frozen state. Each typed question establishes its own hypothetical placement.

For example: "Assume the image appears only at insertion point A. Would this respondent continue reading past section 1 into section 2?" A sibling question asks about insertion point B using the same state. The exact instructions must make the hypothetical location and relevant section boundary unambiguous. The author may also ask whether any placement works, rather than forcing a winner.

Potential dimensions include relevance to nearby text, contribution to understanding, continuity into the next section, interruption or distraction, and expressed interest in continuation. A placement Choice with a no-fit option could complement per-placement judgments. Final questions, rubrics, and reporting are unresolved.

This is a placement assessment made with knowledge of the whole article. It cannot be described as a first encounter in which later sections remain unknown. Questions sharing input are independently authored hypotheticals, but Clef's joint scoring does not establish that their answers are unaffected by sibling questions. Compare batched and separate requests during the spike.

The candidate architectural point is that images can be another frozen input to ask typed questions over, without requiring a new journey-memory mechanism. Within one batch the article and image are supplied once. Separate requests still need the complete relevant input resent.

### Compare rendered visual alternatives over a cohort

The next riff makes appearance itself the question. An author may want to compare typefaces, font sizes, image-left versus image-right placement, or another visible treatment. The user identifies A/B testing two visual things over a cohort as a concrete value slice. Here the frozen stimulus is the rendered visual, not merely article text with an image and a textual placement marker.

Candidate user story: "I have two versions of this article page. Help me understand which presentation better serves these respondents and where they differ." Questions could concern reading comfort, hierarchy, clarity of the next action or section, balance between image and text, perceived tone, and expressed willingness to continue. Choose dimensions that serve the author's decision rather than reducing every design to an attractiveness score. Typeface preference, ability to resolve text, and perceived reading effort are different judgments.

Sheg's matched arms and frozen respondent contexts supply the existing comparison pattern. In a separate-variant study, each respondent context branches into A and B with the same question, criteria, provider/model, and prior state. Each judgment sees its own variant, with no sibling variant or sibling answer leaking into the packet. Compare answers by respondent and question, retaining incomplete and unreached cases. These are modeled comparisons, not a live website conversion experiment.

A direct comparison is a second legitimate study shape: show both A and B together and ask which better serves the specified purpose, with ties or neither handled through authored options where appropriate. Seeing alternatives changes the task, so do not treat that preference judgment as equivalent to separately asking whether each version works. Side-by-side composition, visual labels, and left/right order can influence presentation; keep them explicit and explore order swaps in the spike.

Hold the actual copy and other relevant design conditions fixed when isolating one visual change. Freeze viewport, render scale, capture bounds, font-loading state, and image treatment as part of the stimulus provenance. When font size changes wrapping, article height, or what is visible in the viewport, those are consequences of the treatment and must be recorded rather than silently normalized away. An author can also compare complete redesigns, but the report then concerns the bundle of changes, not an isolated typeface or placement effect.

The model needs sufficient visual evidence of the distinction under study. A screenshot can convey layout that a textual description cannot preserve. Capture resizing, cropping, or downsampling might erase a subtle typeface or size difference. Supplying the copy as separate text may help semantic questions but can let a judgment bypass the readability of the rendered text; specify the evidence appropriate to the question. The spike must establish which appearance judgments Clef can support before promising typography or layout expertise.

Frozen user- or agent-supplied visual variants are a possible first input surface. Automatic browser rendering, capture generation, design editing, gaze maps, and a new visual-comparison runtime are not prerequisites selected by this riff. Reuse the cohort, typed questions, matched arms, and follow-on composition while investigating what visual material contracts and inspection are needed.

## Candidate: mixed-provider reading and visual transitions

A subsequent riff proposes mixing model calls within one logical respondent study. Jev evaluates sections 1 through 3. Only respondents whose recorded path reaches the end of section 3 proceed to a Clef stage. Clef receives each eligible respondent's unchanged profile, compact prior typed decisions with their question/option meanings and encounter order, the actual image, and the header of section 4. It judges whether proceeding into section 4 is appealing. Those routed onward return to Jev for section 4 with their own earlier history and the new typed response from the image transition. Later stages could continue the pattern.

The same respondent ID denotes a stable authored profile and linked evidence across stages, not a persistent mind inside either model. Sheg owns the respondent state, transfer, eligibility, and lineage. Each model evaluates the packet it actually receives. The mixed-provider study must retain which provider made each judgment; combining their outputs does not make their confidence scales interchangeable or their judgments independently corroborated.

This creates another candidate user-value slice: explore whether an image at a transition helps already-engaged modeled respondents choose to continue, then examine the subsequent reading path. Reports need to distinguish the original cohort, respondents reaching the transition, respondents answering the visual question, and respondents routed onward. A positive result applies to the selected reached population, not automatically to everyone who began the article. Comparing alternatives requires preserving the same eligible respondent basis and prior state when the editorial question calls for that comparison.

Two information boundaries are consequential. First, prior choices are not the preceding article content. Clef receiving only profile, compact answers, image, and the next header evaluates an intentionally narrower transition packet; it cannot directly check the image against omitted sections 1 through 3. Supplying earlier text instead would support a richer coherence judgment but restore its context cost. Decide which question requires which representation rather than silently treating compact decisions as a lossless memory of reading.

Second, returning the image-stage answer to Jev carries a recorded reaction. The user proposes that an alt-text-style description of the encountered image also travel in respondent context, carrying some semantics of what was shown alongside what the respondent chose. This is the working direction for the return handoff. It lets later text judgments condition on the image's described content and the respondent's recorded response without requiring Jev to inspect pixels. It does not establish exactly which image details the respondent understood. Questions requiring direct visual inspection still go to a vision-capable provider. Specify this textual representation explicitly rather than silently dropping or replacing material under the existing cumulative-material contract.

A rating or probability does not automatically yield a continued/departed path. An explicit Choice, or an authored routing rule for Score/Noul, must define continuation. No threshold or sampling policy has been selected. Preserve the distinction between a typed judgment, the rule used to route it, and the resulting path.

Current Sheg configuration and execution identity select one provider for a run. The user clarified that agent composition over Sheg's existing durable contexts and follow-ons is the starting point: run to the end of stage 3, select the respondents and exact contexts that reached that point, and repeatedly study that frozen point while varying the image, caption, or other deliberate inputs. The product should make this existing capability useful with visual inputs. A new runtime that switches providers within a single run is not a prerequisite and has not been selected. Existing text-only follow-on mechanics are the composition seam; visual inputs and the proposed textual carry-forward still require extensions and validation.

Hold the reached-respondent selection and pre-image state fixed when exploring variants at the same point. Each alternative branches from that state, not from another alternative's answers. This varies the study input without rerunning sections 1 through 3 or treating repeated identical inputs as additional coverage. A selected visual-stage result can then supply the appropriate respondent-specific context for a Jev follow-on into section 4. Continued and departed sets may differ between alternatives; comparisons must show those differences rather than silently comparing only whichever survivors happen to remain.

### Textual context carried after an image

Keep the image's textual description, the exact caption that accompanied it, and the respondent's typed reaction distinct. The description is a representation of the asset; the caption is authored article content whose wording may itself be varied; the reaction is respondent-specific evidence from the visual-stage question. An illustrative carry-forward is: "Encountered an image depicting a bridge between two isolated cliffs, accompanied by caption X; selected the continue option when asked whether to enter section 4." The profile and earlier decision history continue alongside it.

Tie the description and caption to the exact image variant and encounter so that a later request cannot combine one variant's description with another variant's answer. Descriptions could be author-supplied or prepared by the agent; authorship, preparation, inspection, and final packet shape remain open. Clef returns bounded typed answers, so generating a free-form description is not assumed to be part of its decision response. Do not silently turn author intent or a generated explanation into a claim about the respondent's own interpretation.

The working distinction is between the preserved visual asset and its textual representation for a text-only stage. Keep both available as evidence even when only the textual representation is sent to Jev. A later visual question can explicitly reattach the asset. What is included in the next model packet and what is retained in durable storage are separate choices.

Treat the mixed-provider composition as a candidate to investigate alongside the shared-state placement study. No first implementation scope is approved. Remaining questions concern how the image description is supplied, how the model-visible handoff is framed, and which judgments can reliably use that representation.

## Systems findings and implications

The research discussed here examined published documentation and released code, not live Clef inference. Hosted limits and behavior must be verified before treating them as implementation guarantees.

Clef accepts state, images, and typed Choice, Score, and Noul questions. Its published decision API has no continuation token or persistent image-memory handle. In a cumulative journey, a later call requiring the image's visual evidence needs the actual image resent. A caption, generated description, or saved answer is a textual record, not equivalent visual evidence.

Reattaching a previously encountered image should preserve access to that exposure, not count as a new exposure. The article's encounter order must remain explicit, and future or sibling-only material must remain excluded when the study is a staged journey. The whole-article placement study deliberately has a different visibility contract.

Images travel separately in the request but their processed visual tokens occupy the model input budget alongside text, question schemas, and framing. Clef's released encoder adds media tokens to the prefix and reduces the text allowance accordingly. It places images before serialized state rather than physically interleaving article sections and images. Explicit insertion markers describe narrative order; they do not reproduce the rendered page.

Cloudflare advertises a 65,536-token model window. Its hosted image schema currently permits at most four embedded PNG, JPEG, or WebP images, with per-image byte/pixel limits and a whole-request limit; it rejects remote image URLs. OpenRouter warns that Workers AI currently truncates text state to roughly its first 2,000 tokens. The released local encoder has its own default input bound. Model capacity, hosted preprocessing, and transport limits are separate facts. An accepted HTTP request must not be treated as proof that all article material was read.

Cloudflare documents prefix caching for selected models, but no Clef-specific guarantee was established. Caching may reduce repeated computation; it does not remove the need to supply input or enlarge context. Do not base correctness on cache retention.

## Narrative placement and rendered placement

Two representations serve different questions. Text sections, actual image assets, and explicit insertion points can support judgments about meaning and narrative fit. Rendered captures can additionally provide evidence of size, spacing, typography, prominence, and competition with surrounding content.

The preferred foundation under discussion is ordered, frozen text and visual material, with rendered captures available when the question depends on appearance. This is a recommendation to investigate, not a settled contract or an obligation to build both representations in the first slice.

A whole-page screenshot risks revealing unreached sections in a staged study. Very tall screenshots can make text illegible to the model. Multiple captures can duplicate content and consume the hosted image allowance. Capture boundaries, viewport, scale, asset-to-section binding, and the treatment of captions need explicit decisions. A combined image of multiple alternatives would also change relative scale and presentation; it is not a free substitute for separate variants.

## Fit with Sheg and unresolved contracts

Preserve the distinct typed answer semantics and existing physical-attempt accounting. Provider, route, served model, packet/compiler identity, and exact material must remain identifiable. Adding Clef must not silently label its execution as Jev or change an existing run's provider on resume.

Visual assets need frozen bytes or an equally durable exact representation, stable identity and digests, and independent retention for accepted follow-ons. Extend the delivered v0.3.0 migration and payload-versioning policy where storage or packet contracts change. Do not reinterpret historical text-only runs under new visual semantics.

Open choices include visual material shape and custody, placement definitions, image binding, provider selection, multimodal admission, rendered capture ownership, accepted-input inspection, comparison interpretation, and skill guidance. Preserve the generic respondent-study primitives instead of baking article-specific questions into the runtime.

Static article imagery and rendered visual variants are concrete candidate inputs. The first release scope among these value slices remains open. Video, browser navigation, gaze prediction, simulated forgetting, automated image generation, and fine-tuning have not been selected for this slice. Cumulative replay gives the model renewed access to prior material; it does not reproduce human memory, fatigue, scrolling, or time spent reading.

## Spike questions

- Can Clef bind an actual image to explicit article insertion points and distinguish useful, irrelevant, misleading, or badly timed placements?
- Does changing the image change judgments when article text is held fixed, and do changed surrounding sections affect placement judgments appropriately?
- Are hypothetical instructions respected, including the image appearing only at the specified point and no-placement being a valid outcome?
- How do batched judgments compare with the same questions in separate requests, including reordered sibling questions?
- What text and visual material does the selected hosted route actually admit and read? Check material near the end of long state and quantify resolution, image-count, and question-schema pressure.
- Does a later cumulative judgment retain image-dependent evidence when the image is resent, and how does omission differ? Treat omission as a distinct input, not a memory optimization.
- Do rendered placement and size changes produce useful distinctions beyond textual markers? Which presentation questions remain unsupported or unreliable?
- Can Clef detect the actual visual differences relevant to a rendered A/B question, including a typeface or font-size change, after the selected capture and provider processing? Use obvious and subtle variants to separate basic perceptual feasibility from plausible-sounding preference judgments.
- How do separate-variant cohort judgments compare with a direct A/B preference question? Check presentation-order effects and preserve the distinction between these study shapes.
- For a Jev-to-Clef-to-Jev experiment, do compact decisions carry enough context for the intended transition question, compared with also supplying the preceding article text? Can useful distinctions survive the return handoff when Jev receives the image description, exact caption, and recorded reaction without pixels?
- Can respondent-specific eligibility, exact typed history, question meanings, provider provenance, and safe recovery be preserved across provider boundaries without confusing failed or unanswered stages with departure choices?
- Can an agent branch several image/caption variants from one frozen reached-stage selection without replaying earlier stages, accumulating sibling-variant history, or losing which visual exposure each later textual description and answer represent?

Use bounded fixtures with clear image/text relationships and purposeful variants, not a single attractive example or a broad task-size matrix. Inspect failures and distributions rather than declaring feasibility from one plausible answer. Keep transient spike outputs outside shipped source. No spike has run, and this sketch does not specify its implementation plan or inference budget.
